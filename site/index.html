Research question

How accurately and reliably can occupancy be detected with fewer, lower-cost sensors?

The study compares model performance across sensor combinations, then tests whether those results remain stable when sensor costs, missing readings, noise, drift, and Light failures are considered.

It also examines probability calibration, decision thresholds, and model explanations. The evidence describes the trade-offs in this dataset; it does not establish a final hardware or deployment choice.

Evidence scope

What the analysis establishes

Time-aware performance. Chronological validation and two separate source periods show how model quality changes over time within the studied office.

Explicit trade-offs. Complete sensor ablation and multiple cost scenarios quantify how predictive quality changes as sensors and assumptions change.

Known reliability limits. Fault simulation, routing, calibration, and threshold analysis identify where the candidate is strong and what should be validated prospectively across more rooms and seasons.

Project summary

Strong clean performance, with reliability governed by Light and operating context.

The Temperature + Light + CO2 logistic candidate achieves clean F1 of 0.971 on Test 1 and 0.980 on Test 2 at threshold 0.50. The wider study shows why those scores are not sufficient on their own.

Sensor trade-offLight-containing configurations lead the validation frontier under every tested cost scenario, but the assumed costs do not justify a final hardware recommendation.

Reliability trade-offComplete or plausible Light failures can cause severe degradation. Detector routing offers the strongest tested mitigation overall, while retaining known false alarms and missed faults.

Decision trade-offA threshold of 0.86 reduced validation cost under illustrative assumptions but did not remain advantageous on the held-out periods. Threshold 0.50 remains the stronger observed reference, not a universal optimum.
